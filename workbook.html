Reading a new domain, in five artifacts.

Outsider to value-adding in a review meeting in about six weeks. The sprint produces five things; this workbook is where you build them.

Pre-seeded with a fully-worked public example — higher-ed enrollment marketing, an industry I read for eight years at Sparkroom. Clear it and type your own domain to start fresh. Nothing you type leaves this page — no server, no storage, no tracking. Export to Markdown when you're done.

Week 1–2 · Artifact 1

Glossary tracker

Read one trade article a day; log every term you can't yet use in a sentence. 150–250 entries is the signal vocabulary has landed — when you stop adding new ones on most days, you're done.

Anti-pattern: asking an AI to "summarize the industry." You'll get a plausible summary that fails silently the first time you use it with an insider. Vocabulary has to be absorbed, not summarized.

Week 2–3 · Artifact 2

Money-diagram builder

Who pays whom, for what, on what schedule — with the take-rate at each step. You can't do useful work until you can draw this from memory. The step with the take-rate has the political power.

one page
drawn from memory
Highest-leverage hour in the whole sprint: show a half-drawn version, warts and question marks, to one industry vet and ask them to correct it. People fix diagrams; they won't draw on a blank page. A good coffee is worth three weeks of reading.

Week 3–4 · Artifact 3

Regulator-summary card

Two-to-four rules shape the industry more than the industry does. You don't need to be a lawyer — for each rule, answer one question: what does this prevent a smart actor from doing that they'd otherwise want to do?

Week 4–5 · Artifact 4

Incumbent map

Three-to-seven firms run most of any market. Rank by share; name each one's strategic posture and key vulnerability. Read 10-Ks, Crunchbase, and — the unglamorous winner — former VPs posting on LinkedIn for free. Skip the company websites; they're marketing.

Week 5–6 · Artifact 5

Live-disagreement tracker

What do smart insiders openly disagree about? Find it and you've got two assets: a question that structures everything else you learn, and a conversation opener insiders will actually engage. "What's your take on [live question]?" beats "tell me about your industry" ten to one.
