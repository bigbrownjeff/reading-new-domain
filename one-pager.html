The Domain-Ramp Sprint

From "I don't work in this industry" to "I can hold my own in the review meeting" — in about six weeks, with the artifact to prove it. The standard front door to every engagement.

An incoming exec, operating partner, or functional leader who's never worked the vertical is a liability for a quarter — fluent in the craft, illiterate in the domain. The sprint closes that gap: six weeks of structured reading, two well-chosen coffees, and one artifact that doubles as the test of whether any of it stuck. I've run it on my own account through six domain switches in 30 years; this is the version I run with you, on yours.

The sprint — six activities, one artifact each

The weeks overlap; the activities don't. Vocabulary must land before money is traceable; money before regulation is legible.

Why it's the front door

Most operators dropping into a new vertical already know more than they think — but haven't written it down recently. They can talk for an hour and still not draw the money diagram from memory or name the live disagreement out loud. The sprint surfaces those gaps fast and produces a shared artifact the whole team can argue from. Every deeper engagement I run — the model, the platform call, the channel-mix work — is better when it starts here, because it starts from an honest map instead of a vibe.

What you walk away with

  • A glossary of the 150–250 terms that buy you membership.
  • A one-page money diagram, take-rates filled in.
  • A regulator summary: the 2–4 rules and what each prevents a smart actor from doing.
  • An incumbent map: the 3–7 firms, ranked by share, posture, vulnerability.
  • The live disagreement, your current stance, and what would update it.
  • A ranked list of the three things you still can't explain — the honest gaps.
